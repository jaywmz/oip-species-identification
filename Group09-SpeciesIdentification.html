Species Identification Project

Empowering Citizen Science at Glasgow Botanic Gardens

Project Overview

Glasgow City Council (GCC) is committed to its Biodiversity Action Plan, requiring accurate and regular data on plant and wildlife species across the city. The Glasgow Botanic Gardens, a key site, hosts both cultivated and native species. Currently, species identification and data entry are largely manual, leading to inefficiencies.

This project aims to streamline the process of recording biological sightings, reduce manual data entry, and expand citizen participation through the iNaturalist app, ensuring continuous data collection year-round.

The Problem

The current method for adding species to the biological record is ad-hoc and involves manual entry into spreadsheets after expert authentication. This process is time-consuming and limits the frequency and volume of data collection.

  • Manual Data Entry: Significant time spent on manual transcription.
  • Ad-hoc Reporting: Data collection is not continuous or systematic.
  • Limited Citizen Participation: Reliance on experts for authentication slows down the process and restricts broader engagement beyond events like the City Nature Challenge.

Our Design Thinking Process

Our approach to solving this challenge followed a human-centered design thinking methodology, ensuring the solution is both effective and user-friendly.

1. Empathize

We began by understanding the needs of all stakeholders: citizen scientists, local authorities (GCC), and biodiversity experts. We researched existing data collection methods, the iNaturalist app, and other identification services to identify pain points and opportunities.

2. Define

Based on our research, we defined the core problem: how to streamline species data collection and authentication while expanding citizen participation year-round, reducing manual effort.

3. Ideate

We brainstormed various solutions, focusing on leveraging existing tools like iNaturalist and integrating new functionalities. Ideas included automated data sync, expert review workflows, and user-friendly submission interfaces.

4. Prototype

We developed a conceptual model for an integrated system, outlining the user journey from observation to authenticated record. This involved mapping out data flows and potential user interface elements.

5. Test & Iterate

While this is a static demonstration, the next phase would involve testing prototypes with users and experts, gathering feedback, and iteratively refining the solution to ensure it meets the project goals effectively.

Proposed Solution & Key Features

Our proposed solution centers on integrating citizen science efforts with expert validation through a streamlined digital workflow, building upon the strengths of the iNaturalist platform.

  • iNaturalist Integration: Encourage and leverage iNaturalist for primary data collection by citizen recorders. Observations made within the Glasgow Botanic Gardens can be easily tagged or submitted to a dedicated project.
  • Automated Data Sync: Develop a mechanism (or utilize iNaturalist API capabilities) to automatically pull relevant observations from iNaturalist into a central database, reducing manual spreadsheet entry.
  • Expert Review Dashboard: Create a web-based dashboard for experts to review, authenticate, and categorize submitted observations efficiently. This dashboard would display key information (photos, location, proposed ID) and allow for quick approval or feedback.
  • Feedback Loop for Recorders: Implement a system to provide feedback to citizen scientists on their submissions, fostering learning and improving data quality over time.
  • Enhanced Data Reporting: Automate the generation of reports for GCC's Biodiversity Action Plan, leveraging the structured data collected.
  • User-Friendly Interface: Ensure any custom interfaces (e.g., expert dashboard) are intuitive and easy to navigate, minimizing training requirements.
